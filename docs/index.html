Mandos

A multi-model deliberation MCP server. A panel of models answers your question, a calibrated decision model judges what they established, and your own model writes the answer.

Named for the Doomsman of the Valar, who pronounces judgement.

How it works

The usual design has a second chat model read the panel and write a summary. That summary has the same failure modes as the answers it is grading, and it states its confidence in words.

Mandos gives the judging to Jev, TypeSafe's decision model. Jev answers named questions in fixed shapes: a probability for a yes/no question, a label with a distribution for a choice, a level on a rubric for a score. A consensus in the output therefore means something specific. Every panel member scored above 0.6 on that claim, and the scores are returned with it.

The judge also reports what the panel lacked. When answering needs a row count or a query plan that none of the models had, that comes back as a ranked list, each item scored for how likely it is to change the answer. The calling model fetches what it can reach and asks again.

Install

# macOS / Linux
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fox-Islam/mandos/main/scripts/install.sh | sh

# Windows
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fox-Islam/mandos/main/scripts/install.ps1 | iex

The script installs uv if absent, resolves Python 3.13, and puts two commands on your PATH: mandos (the configurator) and mandos-mcp (the stdio server your harness spawns). Then:

mandos

The configurator sets up your panel, picks the judge, writes ~/.mandos/config.json and ~/.mandos/.env (0600), and wires Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode. Invoke it with /council.

Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint works as a panel member, so vLLM, Ollama and LM Studio need no special handling. mandos doctor labels each provider on-prem or off-prem so you can see what leaves the machine.

Benchmark

The benchmark tests one thing. When answering correctly depends on a fact none of the models has, does a panel do better than a single model?

Setup

16 questions. Each is underdetermined until one specific fact arrives, and each fact is chosen so that the plausible answer without it is wrong. Answers are a forced Yes or No and are graded against a key.

Q: Our dashboard runs SELECT COUNT(*) FROM organisations on every page
   load and we are considering replacing it with a counter table
   maintained by triggers. Is that worth doing?

Withheld: organisations contains 1,180 rows and grows by ~20 a month.
          The COUNT(*) executes in 0.4ms.

Correct:  No

Within a tier, solo and panel use the same author model, so the panel, the judge and the evidence loop are the only things that vary.

The withheld fact is supplied only when the judge asked for it, and whether it asked is graded by Jev rather than by keyword matching. A judge that does not ask gets no second pass, which is the constraint a real caller works under.

Prompts

Every answer ends with a forced verdict line, so grading needs no second model:

Reason briefly, then end with a single line of exactly the form
'FINAL: Yes' or 'FINAL: No'. You must commit to one; 'it depends'
is not an answer.

Solo — the baseline, one call, no panel:

Answer the question. {ANSWER_RULE}

Panel member — the shipped panel prompt:

Answer independently and thoroughly. Do not refer to other panelists.
If answering well needs information you have not been given, say
plainly what is missing and what you are assuming in its place, then
answer under that assumption. Name the measurement that would settle
it, not the topic. {ANSWER_RULE}

Author — writes the final answer from the analysis:

A panel was asked this question and a judge analysed their answers.
You are the author: decide yourself from the analysis and the raw
answers. The panel members are advisors, not voters, and can be
confidently and unanimously wrong. Where the analysis carries numbers,
weight findings by them rather than by how confidently they are
phrased. {ANSWER_RULE}

Claim extraction — the generative half of the hybrid judge, which proposes what Jev then decides. Abridged:

You read answers from a panel of independent models and list what is
worth checking. You do NOT judge, rank, merge, or answer the question
yourself.
- claims: the substantive propositions the answers make...
- blind_spots: things a careful reader would expect an answer to
  address that no answer addresses...
- missing_evidence: facts that are not in the question and that would
  change the answer if they came back one way rather than another.
  Ask yourself what you would have to measure to be sure, and list
  that... Name the measurement, not the subject. Propose generously —
  each one is scored afterwards and the weak ones are dropped.
Output STRICT JSON only.

Solo is the author model on its own. Panel is the full loop. Asked counts how often the judge named the withheld fact, which decides whether a second pass happens.

Conclusion

The panel recovered all 8 questions the solo baselines got wrong, and lost 1 it should have had. Where the two disagreed:

Panel right, solo wrong:

  • index_selectivity — frontier
  • ci_parallel — budget
  • ci_parallel — frontier
  • cache_p99 — frontier
  • read_replica — budget
  • read_replica — frontier
  • code_split — budget
  • code_split — frontier

Solo right, panel wrong:

  • kafka_partitions — budget

The gain comes from the evidence request more than from the panel. Where the judge named the missing fact, the second pass had it and answered correctly. Where it did not ask, no second pass ran and the answer rested on the first.

The frontier tier gained more because its author started lower, at 11/16 against the budget author's 13/16. These questions reward a model that stops and asks over one that reasons carefully from incomplete premises, and a stronger model builds a more convincing case for the wrong answer. Its panel recovered all of them.

The two tiers cost $3.15 in total. Jev is a small part of that, at roughly $0.0002 to adjudicate a whole panel.

Caveats

  • 16 questions, one repeat. A one- or two-question gap is not a result.
  • The questions are built so that a missing fact decides the answer, which flatters any method that asks for evidence. They say nothing about questions where the panel already has what it needs and disagrees anyway.
  • Answers are binary and graded automatically, so nothing here measures the quality of an explanation.
  • The frontier tier used claude-opus-4.8 and gpt-5.5, both a generation behind claude-fable-5.1 and gpt-5.6-sol, so that row shows a strong panel rather than the strongest available.
  • The panel has no web access and cannot read your files. Evidence reaches it because the calling model gathers it.
  • This is not OpenRouter Fusion's benchmark. Theirs is DRACO: 100 deep-research tasks, rubric-graded, with web search and a shell available to every model. None of that is reproducible here.
